Edge unfoldings with at most six vertices: proof status

A net is a non-overlapping edge unfolding (touching counts as non-overlapping). This page shows how the theorem splits into combinatorial types, which cases are settled and why, and exactly what remains open for the octahedron. Every figure is a real unfolding computed from a real polytope.

Current position · 10 September 2026 JST. Four of the seven six-vertex types are settled here. The octahedron, octahedron minus an edge, and prism with one diagonal remain open in general.

Previous proof step: 7 of the 28 minus-edge failure classes are now excluded for every convex realization, using Pinciu’s face-neighborhood theorem. The two-choice unfolding rule remains conjectural, with 21 classes open. The earlier one-tree, 16-cell certificate still covers the same coordinate box.

Latest advance, 11 September: the selected net is proved safe when the sharpest curvature is at least 180°. This covers all 28 face pairs, including equality. Below 180°, a nonobtuse pole angle or a short-radius argument proves more families. The remaining target needs an obtuse pole angle and a gap-crossing angle below 90°. See what is proved and what remains open.

This is a proved reduction; the two universal checks remain open. See the figure, solved cases, and remaining work.

Previous proof, 11 September: Lemma L is proved. The cut-edge separator closes the slit route, and a new convexity/pole-triangle argument closes the route through the fan. All three local pairs are safe under the stated selections. The Lemma L page now gives the complete solved-case table and identifies the remaining Lemma F obligations. Previous proof, directly on Lemma L: curvature now separates the two possible local overlap routes. At a combined curvature of exactly 180°, all three local pairs are proved safe. Below that threshold, a remaining overlap must involve one inward-facing petal; above it, only the route through the fan remains. Those were open at that stage; the new complete proof above now settles them. The progress panel now records these branches separately. Earlier results stay in the collapsible history.
