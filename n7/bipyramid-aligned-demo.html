One tree throughout the insertion

Raise a new pole from the base of a pentagonal pyramid. The same six cuts stay safe.

The poles line up above and below the same interior point of the equator. The old pole stays fixed; the new one rises to a smaller height. The written theorem covers every height in this interval. Slider frames and displayed angles are rounded illustrations.

Proved throughout the full insertion by an auxiliary-pyramid comparison.

Why the proof still works as the pole rises

The green outline is a convex polygon. A pyramid on that polygon has lateral triangles with exactly the lengths of the original gold petals. Its known safe star unfolding contains the whole original net.

Scope of this result

The proof covers arbitrary convex planar equators and arbitrary positive heights when the poles line up perpendicularly through an interior point. It does not yet cover arbitrary pentagonal bipyramids with displaced poles or nonplanar equators. This page illustrates one family already proved for every height; it does not claim that 101 drawings certify an unexamined continuum.
