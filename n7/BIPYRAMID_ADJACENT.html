The final two-inward case: adjacent patches

Two adjacent inward patches: the final two-patch case

13 September 2026 · Complete written proof. The adjacent same-direction class FFCCC, canonical BBCCC, is now covered. Consequently every convex pentagonal bipyramid with at most two inward patches has a nonoverlapping edge unfolding. The remaining symbolic classes have three or four inward patches: eight classes, with their large-pole and aligned-pole families already proved.

The whole pentagonal bipyramid remains open. The argument below is not a numerical extrapolation, and it does not require a pole-curvature order. In fact this adjacent-run theorem works for any ring size when these are the only two inward patches.

What makes this case different

Both bad patches lean forward, one immediately after the other. The first therefore points into another bad patch, so the convex-receiver theorem cannot be applied to that first pair directly.

Instead, compare where the two patches end around a pole. If the first ends earlier and the second reaches only its convex neighbor, the net is safe. If that ordering fails, the second bad patch fits inside the first patch’s triangular indentation. Its pole copy is then unusually close to the fan center. That distance bound is precisely what the radial argument needs.

1. The two comparisons and the finite rule

Normalize the pattern to Q0=F,Q1=FQ_0=F,Q_1=F, with every other patch convex. Write ℓis\ell_i^s for bad patch ii’s extension at fan pole ss, and ωis\omega_i^s for its fan-face angle. Put

As=ℓ0s−ω1s−ℓ1s,Bs=ℓ1s−ω2s.A_s=\ell_0^s-\omega_1^s-\ell_1^s, \qquad B_s=\ell_1^s-\omega_2^s.

Thus As≤0A_s\le0 means the first bad patch ends no later than the second; Bs≤0B_s\le0 means the second bad patch’s extension is short into Q2Q_2.

The angle identities imply

Ap+Aq=−κu1<0,Bp+Bq<0.A_p+A_q=-\kappa_{u_1}<0,\qquad B_p+B_q<0.

The second inequality is the usual convex-receiver identity. For the first, write the two inward corner excesses as ρ0,ρ1\rho_0,\rho_1. The corner sum at u1u_1 and full angle sum of Q1Q_1 give

ρ0−ρ1=ω1p+ω1q−κu1.\rho_0-\rho_1=\omega_1^p+\omega_1^q-\kappa_{u_1}.

Substitute ρi=ℓip+ℓiq\rho_i=\ell_i^p+\ell_i^q.

Theorem Adjacent. The following rule gives a safe edge unfolding.

  1. Choose a pole qq with Bq≤0B_q\le0.
  2. If Aq≤0A_q\le0, use qq as fan.
  3. Otherwise Ap<0A_p<0 at the other pole. If Bp≤0B_p\le0, use pp instead.
  4. In the remaining situation, keep qq as fan. The second bad patch is short there and long at the other pole; the proof below handles it.

In every branch, slit at u0u_0: cut that one fan edge and all the edges at the other pole. For a pentagonal bipyramid these are six original edges. Equalities in the two short/ordering tests are included.

2. Two useful containment facts

Let OO be the chosen fan center, a=u1a=u_1, and P0,P1P_0,P_1 the source copies of the two bad patches. Since the second patch is inward at its far vertex u2u_2, it lies in its outer triangle

H1=conv{O,a,P1}.H_1=\operatorname{conv}\{O,a,P_1\}.

The first bad patch is safe against the entire triangle H1H_1. At the shared, uncut vertex aa, this larger triangle has exactly the same angular corner as the two faces of Q1Q_1. The two faces of Q0Q_0 and that corner of H1H_1 form consecutive sectors of total angle 2π−κa<2π2\pi-\kappa_a<2\pi. Filling the missing part at u2u_2 therefore introduces no overlap with Q0Q_0.

A convex receiver, where it enters the preceding bad cone, stays in that bad patch’s outer triangle. Apply this to Q2Q_2 and Q1Q_1. The receiver is convex and contains OO. A radial segment from OO to an interior receiver point in the extension sector cannot pass through the open cut side u2P1u_2P_1: immediately beyond that side lies the bad petal of Q1Q_1, which is safe against Q2Q_2 by the uncut fan at u2u_2. Thus those receiver points lie in the indentation conv{O,u2,P1}⊂H1\operatorname{conv}\{O,u_2,P_1\}\subset H_1.

This statement is used for interior overlap. A boundary ray that merely touches the cone need not be treated as a strict containment assertion; it cannot create an interior overlap. The argument is valid whether the bad extension is short or long.

3. The ordered, short case

Suppose As≤0A_s\le0 and Bs≤0B_s\le0 at the chosen fan pole. With slit u0u_0, the angular endpoints satisfy

ω0s+ℓ0s≤ω0s+ω1s+ℓ1s≤ω0s+ω1s+ω2s.\omega_0^s+\ell_0^s \le\omega_0^s+\omega_1^s+\ell_1^s \le\omega_0^s+\omega_1^s+\omega_2^s.

The first bad patch cannot reach any patch beyond Q2Q_2; the second reaches only its own receiver Q2Q_2. The adjacent pairs Q0/Q1Q_0/Q_1 and Q1/Q2Q_1/Q_2 are safe by their uncut equator vertices.

Any possible interior overlap Q0/Q2Q_0/Q_2 lies within the cone of Q1Q_1, because the first bad patch ends no later than that cone. The clipped receiver fact puts the overlap in H1H_1, which is already safe against Q0Q_0. This is a contradiction. All further patches are convex and have disjoint fan wedges. Neither bad patch extends across the slit. The entire net is safe.

4. When the ordering fails

The only remaining branch of the rule has

Aq>0,Bq<0,Ap<0,Bp>0.A_q>0,\quad B_q<0,\quad A_p<0,\quad B_p>0.

Use qq as fan. The inequality Aq>0A_q>0 says that the whole outer cone of Q1Q_1 fits into Q0Q_0’s extension sector:

ω1q+ℓ1q<ℓ0q.\omega_1^q+\ell_1^q<\ell_0^q.

The source copy P1P_1 is therefore strictly inside the indentation

T0=conv{a,q,P0}.T_0=\operatorname{conv}\{a,q,P_0\}.

For clarity, the cone condition controls the side qP0qP_0; the positive uncut angle at aa places P1P_1 between the other two sides. Also |aP1|=|aP0||aP_1|=|aP_0| because these are copies of the same original edge.

Let

D1=|qP1|,Δ0=dist(q,[aP0]).D_1=|qP_1|,\qquad \Delta_0=\operatorname{dist}(q,[aP_0]).

The unit-circle argument in the shield proof already proves the intermediate bound

D1<Δ0.D_1<\Delta_0.

This part uses only the strict pocket containment and equal radii; it does not require Q1Q_1 to be convex. The angle argument in the long-receiver proof likewise gives

ℓ0q<π−κa2<π2.\ell_0^q<\frac{\pi-\kappa_a}{2}<\frac\pi2.

Here it uses only the positive corner of Q1Q_1 at aa, not convexity at its other endpoint. Explicitly, the circle gives ℓ0p>(π−κa)/2\ell_0^p>(\pi-\kappa_a)/2, while the corner at aa gives ℓ0p+ℓ0q<π−κa\ell_0^p+\ell_0^q<\pi-\kappa_a.

At the other pole pp, Bp>0B_p>0 means that Q1Q_1 has a long convex receiver Q2Q_2. That receiver’s source angle at qq is obtuse. Therefore

ω2q>π2>ℓ0q.\omega_2^q>\frac\pi2>\ell_0^q.

This prevents Q0Q_0 from reaching any patch beyond Q2Q_2 in the chosen qq-fan. The only nonadjacent pair still needing attention is Q0/Q2Q_0/Q_2.

5. The distance barrier closes that last pair

Apply the shield lemma to the long Q1Q_1 at the other fan pole pp. Its receiver’s far equator vertex is u3u_3. Write d3=|pu3|+|qu3|d_3=|pu_3|+|qu_3|. The distance from pp to that bad cut segment is at most its distance to the segment’s source endpoint, which is D1D_1. The developed pole-to-pole length of the same patch does not depend on which pole is chosen as fan. Hence

d3<D1<Δ0.d_3<D_1<\Delta_0.

Now apply the cone-and-disk lemma to that same long receiver, viewing it in the chosen qq-fan. It places Q2Q_2 in the union of the bad Q1Q_1 cone and the radius-d3d_3 disk centered at qq.

Inside the cone, an interior overlap with Q0Q_0 would lie in H1H_1 by the clipped receiver fact, and Q0Q_0 is safe against H1H_1. Outside that cone, Q2Q_2 lies in the disk. The protruding part of Q0Q_0 cannot enter it: every such point has radius at least Δ0>d3\Delta_0>d_3. Its own fan wedge cannot meet Q2Q_2 either.

Thus Q0/Q2Q_0/Q_2 is safe. The adjacent pairs were already safe, and the angular bound in the previous section excluded every farther pair. This proves the whole selected net and completes the theorem.

What is now left

Every necessary patch class with zero, one, or two inward patches is covered: 7 of 15 classes in total. The remaining eight consist of five three-inward and three four-inward patterns. All cases with a pole curvature at least 180∘180^\circ are already proved; the full aligned-pole family is also proved at arbitrary heights.

The next task is to extend these containment and distance arguments to three inward patches, where a patch may have to pass through a longer run before reaching a convex receiver. The whole graph remains open.

Exact applications independently check all face pairs of the chosen nets and the strict comparisons used in those instances. The replay checker separates those finite checks from the universal written proof. Boundary equality cases are covered by the argument above. No numerical search is a proof premise, and no Lean formalization is claimed.
