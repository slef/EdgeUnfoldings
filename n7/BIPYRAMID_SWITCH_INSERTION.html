Growing the seventh vertex with finitely many safe slit changes

13 September 2026 · A proved insertion family. Start with a pentagonal pyramid, insert a pole at zero curvature in its base, and move it out to its final position. A version of the curvature-zero idea that can be proved

Let a convex polygon UU lie in a plane, and let a convex bipyramid have poles q,p1q,p_1 on opposite sides. Let cc be the intersection of the segment qp1qp_1 with the polygon’s plane. It is strictly inside UU: every ring edge supports both pole fans, so the intersection is on the interior side of every polygon edge.

Keep qq and UU fixed, and set

p(t)=c+t(p1−c),0≤t≤1.p(t)=c+t(p_1-c),\qquad 0\le t\le1.

Assume the plane through qq perpendicular to qp1qp_1 supports the solid at qq:

(ui−q)⋅(p1−q)≥0for every ring vertex.(1)(u_i-q)\cdot(p_1-q)\ge0\qquad\text{for every ring vertex}. \qquad(1)

Theorem Finite-Switch Insertion. For every t>0t>0, cut the star at qq and the edge from p(t)p(t) to a maximizer of

di(t)=|qui|+|p(t)ui|.d_i(t)=|qu_i|+|p(t)u_i|.

The resulting edge net is safe. The selected slit can be kept constant between at most m(m−1)m(m-1) parameter values for an mm-vertex equator. At a tie, either maximizing slit is safe. Thus an insertion with five ring vertices needs at most 20 possible change values.

The pole axis may be tilted relative to the base. This extends the earlier aligned-pole insertion, which retained one fixed slit in its entire family.

Why every intermediate solid and net are valid

For t>0t>0 the prescribed two pole fans are exactly the faces of a convex bipyramid. For each moving face, the supporting determinants of the other ring vertices scale linearly with tt from zero, with the strict signs at t=1t=1. Its determinant against the fixed pole qq is affine in tt and has the same strict sign at both endpoints, including the base triangle at t=0t=0. For each fixed face, the moving pole stays strictly on its interior side by convexity of that half-space. All triangles are nondegenerate because cc lies strictly inside the polygon.

The vectors p(t)−qp(t)-q are positive multiples of p1−qp_1-q. Therefore (1) holds with the moving pole for every tt, and the Source Plane theorem proves the selected net safe pointwise throughout the interval.

At t=0t=0 the new marked point cc has zero curvature. Its fan triangles reassemble the original undivided polygonal base, and the two copies of the formal slit coincide. The net is the ordinary safe edge-star unfolding of the six-vertex pyramid; no extra cut through its base is needed in that limiting solid.

The inserted curvature is nondecreasing. Its normal cones are nested as the point moves away from cc: if a normal supports the moving point at time t1>0t_1>0, its dot product with p1−cp_1-c is nonnegative, and it still supports that point at every later time. Their spherical areas are the vertex curvatures. Conversely, old normal cones shrink as the hull grows, so old curvatures do not increase. In particular, if the inserted vertex is least curved at t=1t=1, it is least curved throughout this insertion.

Why only finitely many slit changes are needed

Write si=|qui|s_i=|qu_i| and Ai(t)=|p(t)ui|2A_i(t)=|p(t)u_i|^2. Every AiA_i is a positive quadratic polynomial with the same quadratic coefficient.

A tie between did_i and djd_j says

Ai(t)−Aj(t)=sj−si=δ.\sqrt{A_i(t)}-\sqrt{A_j(t)}=s_j-s_i=\delta.

If δ=0\delta=0, this reduces to the linear equation Ai−Aj=0A_i-A_j=0, unless the two functions are identical. If δ≠0\delta\ne0, every tie satisfies

(Ai−Aj−δ2)2=4δ2Aj.(A_i-A_j-\delta^2)^2=4\delta^2 A_j.

This polynomial has degree at most two because Ai−AjA_i-A_j is affine. When it is not identically zero, there are at most two candidate values. Squaring can add candidates but cannot remove a real tie.

If the polynomial is identically zero, the positive square roots are analytic on the interval. Factoring the squared identity shows that one of their signed sums or differences is identically δ\delta or −δ-\delta. Either the desired difference is identically δ\delta, or it never equals it; positivity excludes isolated intersections with a wrong sum branch. Identically tied vertices can always use a fixed index. Thus every nonidentical pair contributes at most two tie values.

There are (m2)\binom m2 pairs. Between all those candidate values the maximizing index cannot change, proving the bound 2(m2)=m(m−1)2\binom m2=m(m-1). This bounds the number of possible changes, not the numerical effort needed to decide exact algebraic comparisons in a particular implementation.

Scope

This proves a complete insertion family, including an explicit minimum- curvature example. It does not yet handle every pentagonal bipyramid or every minimum-curvature insertion. The old failure examples and earlier proofs are retained. Whole counts remain 7/15 bipyramid patch classes and 10/34 seven-vertex graph types.
