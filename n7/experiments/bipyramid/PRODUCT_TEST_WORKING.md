# Local diagonal-product criterion — proof draft

Fan p, source q, L=|pq|, H_a=|pa|²−|qa|². At an inward ring
corner a let D_−,D_+ be the two developed pole diagonals of its incident
patches. A sufficient condition for ordered source rays, and hence a safe
max-distance-sum slit, is

H_a ≤ D_− D_+ for every inward corner a.

At the opposite source, H changes sign and D are unchanged. Thus the
criterion misses both poles exactly when some inward corners have
H_a>D_−D_+ and some have H_b<−D_−D_+. Failed sufficient tests are not
counterexamples to a net or to unfolding existence.

Proof of sufficiency: an angular reversal at any relevant F/F or F/C
junction captures M in triangle(a,p,P), equal copied radii s=|aq|.
Set D=|pP|, E=|pM|; capture gives E<D. With a at angle0 from p,
P and M have angles 0<β<α<π (reflect a B junction). Put r=|pa|.
The cosine rule gives

cos α−cos β = (D−E)/(2r) * (1−H_a/(DE)).

The left side is negative, D−E positive, so H_a>DE. Contradiction.
E<D follows directly from circle normalization a0,P=s angle0,
p=r angleδ,M=s angleκ with 0<κ<δ<π:
E²=r²+s²−2rs cos(δ−κ)<r²+s²−2rs cosδ=D².

All other neighboring patch types are ordered directly. The source-plane
proof's complete pair enumeration then applies unchanged.

The geometric source-plane criterion only requires H_a≤L² at inward
corners, since D_−D_+>L². Thus only those ring vertices need be behind
q's plane. Every still-open geometry must contain inward corners projecting
beyond both pole endpoints. A whole-ring plane remains the simplest
sufficient corollary.
