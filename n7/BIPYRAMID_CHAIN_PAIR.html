1. The one independent pair and its notation

Take pattern FCCBC and choose the fan pole pp by the combined-extension rule. Let qq be the other pole. Suppose Q0Q_0 is the long patch:

ℓ0p>ω1,ℓ0p+ℓ3p<ω1+ω2.\ell_0^p>\omega_1,\qquad \ell_0^p+\ell_3^p<\omega_1+\omega_2.

Here ωi\omega_i are the face angles at the chosen fan pole. Slit at pu0p u_0. The only independent question is whether petals V0,V2V_0,V_2 overlap; its solution also separates V0/W2V_0/W_2 by the boundary argument. Reflection handles a long Q3Q_3 with middle patch Q2Q_2 instead.

K=κa+κb,α=ϕ+κa,β=ψ+κb,θ=α+β=π−ν+K.K=\kappa_a+\kappa_b,\qquad \alpha=\phi+\kappa_a,\quad\beta=\psi+\kappa_b,\quad \theta=\alpha+\beta=\pi-\nu+K.

2. A long receiver extension excludes the small-fan pocket

Write the inward corner of Q0Q_0 at aa as π+η\pi+\eta. Let hh be W0W_0’s angle at aa. Since the other part of that corner is the triangular angle a0<πa_0<\pi,

h+a0=π+η⇒h>η.h+a_0=\pi+\eta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h>\eta.

The two-pole extension identity gives 0<ℓ0p<η0<\ell_0^p<\eta. The long-extension hypothesis therefore implies

h>η>ℓ0p>ω1.h>\eta>\ell_0^p>\omega_1.

Write x,yx,y for the angles at a,ba,b in W1W_1, and z>0z>0 for W2W_2’s angle at bb. As x+y=π−ω1x+y=\pi-\omega_1,

σ+τ=h+x+y+z=π+(h−ω1)+z>π.\sigma+\tau=h+x+y+z =\pi+(h-\omega_1)+z>\pi.

Consequently the small-fan branch σ+τ<π\sigma+\tau<\pi is impossible. There is no need to extend the n6 pocket-containment argument in this receiver problem. This is a universal angle deduction, not a search result.

The receiver’s source angle must be obtuse

There is also a useful geometric obstruction to a long extension. Put T=conv{a,p,P}T=\operatorname{conv}\{a,p,P\}, where PP is the bad patch’s source copy. Its side aPaP is an actual boundary edge of the bad petal. The receiver fan triangle W1W_1 starts inside TT near pp. Its angular wedge has width ω1<ℓ0p\omega_1<\ell_0^p, so it cannot leave through side pPpP; side papa is its own boundary. It cannot cross the open side aPaP, because W1W_1 and the bad petal belong to the same uncut vertex fan at aa. Consequently W1⊂TW_1\subset T, with its open base abab inside TT.

The attached receiver petal V1V_1 starts inside TT along abab. Convexity of Q1Q_1 confines it to the same fan wedge, so the two radial sides cannot be crossed. The remaining side aPaP is protected by the same uncut-vertex separation. Thus V1⊂TV_1\subset T too. The two copied cut edges at aa have the positive curvature gap κa\kappa_a, so its apex MM is not on aPaP; it is strictly inside TT.

Let a=0a=0 and e=M/|M|e=M/|M|. Since |M|=|P|=s|M|=|P|=s, the functional e⋅ze\cdot z takes values ss at MM, 00 at aa, and scosκa<ss\cos\kappa_a<s at PP. The strict interior containment forces e⋅p>se\cdot p>s. Hence

|ap|2>|aM|2+|pM|2.|ap|^2>|aM|^2+|pM|^2.

The angle aMpaMp is obtuse. Because pMpM is the diagonal of the convex receiver, its source apex angle ν\nu includes this angle; therefore ν>π/2\nu>\pi/2 as well. Also the intrinsic diagonal through Q1Q_1 has length |pM|≥|pq||pM|\ge|pq|. The original triangle apqapq consequently has an obtuse angle at qq, and the spherical-link bound gives κq<π\kappa_q<\pi.

Thus taking a pole of curvature at least π\pi as source makes every bad extension into a convex receiver short. For two distinct receivers, this supplies a shorter alternative proof of the earlier large-pole branch. The general weighted-curvature argument is still useful when both poles have smaller curvature.

3. The whole large-angle branch is safe

Suppose θ≥π\theta\ge\pi. The two outer petals lie in their cones at a,ba,b. With the preceding orientation, the left cone and the reversed right cone have direction intervals

[α,2π−σ],[τ,2π−β].[\alpha,2\pi-\sigma],\qquad [\tau,2\pi-\beta].

Both lie in the sector

S=[min(α,τ),2π−min(σ,β)].S=[\min(\alpha,\tau),\,2\pi-\min(\sigma,\beta)].

Its width is at most π\pi. Indeed each of α+σ\alpha+\sigma, β+τ\beta+\tau exceeds π\pi by the vertex sums; α+β=θ≥π\alpha+\beta=\theta\ge\pi; and σ+τ>π\sigma+\tau>\pi was just proved. Thus the sum of the two minima is at least π\pi. The sector excludes the positive base direction.

A point in both petals would give b−a=(point−a)−(point−b)b-a=(\text{point}-a)-(\text{point}-b). The two summands belong to the left cone and reversed right cone, so their sum would belong to the convex sector SS. The nonzero vector b−ab-a points along the excluded positive base direction, a contradiction. Boundary equality at θ=π\theta=\pi causes no interior overlap.

This settles the remaining petal pair, hence the entire selected net, throughout the large-angle branch.

4. The small-angle branch has an explicit separator

Now 0<θ<π0<\theta<\pi. The copied cut rays aP,bRaP,bR have directions α\alpha and π−β\pi-\beta, and meet at a point XX above the base. Write

L=|aX|,T=|bX|,s=|aM|=|aP|,t=|bM|=|bR|.L=|aX|,\quad T=|bX|,\quad s=|aM|=|aP|,\quad t=|bM|=|bR|.

The middle apex is strictly inside triangle abXabX: its base angles ϕ,ψ\phi,\psi are strictly less than α,β\alpha,\beta. The convex function z↦|az|+|bz|z\mapsto |az|+|bz| takes value dd at the two base endpoints and the strictly larger value L+TL+T at XX. Therefore

s+t<L+T.s+t<L+T.

At least one copied cut edge is short, meaning s<Ls<L or t<Tt<T.

Wider-apex separator. Either of the following is sufficient:

s≤L and λ≤π+θ2,t≤T and ρ≤π+θ2.s\le L\ \text{ and }\ \lambda\le\frac{\pi+\theta}{2}, \qquad t\le T\ \text{ and }\ \rho\le\frac{\pi+\theta}{2}.

In particular, if both outer apex angles satisfy the displayed bound, the pair is safe. No auxiliary hull or curvature ranking is required.

Here is the planar argument, included to identify its exact hypotheses. The sum-of-angles condition

λ+ρ<π+θ\lambda+\rho<\pi+\theta

is automatic: λ+ν+ρ\lambda+\nu+\rho is only part of the source’s face-angle sum 2π−κq2\pi-\kappa_q, whereas π+θ=2π−ν+K\pi+\theta=2\pi-\nu+K. Let e,fe,f be unit vectors at angles π+α\pi+\alpha and π−β\pi-\beta. Then

R−P=(s−L)e+(t−T)f.R-P=(s-L)e+(t-T)f.

Consider the left short-edge condition. If λ≤θ\lambda\le\theta, the linear functional extracting the ee coefficient in the basis e,fe,f is nonnegative on the left apex cone and on the reversed right apex cone. Its value on R−PR-P is s−L≤0s-L\le0. Equality confines any common points to a supporting line; face interiors are disjoint.

If θ<λ≤(π+θ)/2\theta<\lambda\le(\pi+\theta)/2, put gg at angle π+α−λ\pi+\alpha-\lambda. Both cones lie counterclockwise of gg in a sector of width λ+max(ρ−θ,0)<π\lambda+\max(\rho-\theta,0)<\pi. Writing A=L−s≥0A=L-s\ge0 and B=t−T<AB=t-T<A gives

det(g,R−P)=−Asinλ+Bsin(λ−θ)<0.\det(g,R-P)=-A\sin\lambda+B\sin(\lambda-\theta)<0.

If B≤0B\le0, strictness follows directly. If B>0B>0, use B<AB<A and sinλ≥sin(λ−θ)\sin\lambda\ge\sin(\lambda-\theta), which follows from the stated upper bound on λ\lambda. This strictly separates the translated cones. Reflection proves the right short-edge condition.

This is the local wider-angle argument from the n6 shared core, with its sufficient hypotheses retained explicitly. It does not import the n6 Gauss–Bonnet shortcut.

5. Exactly how the seventh vertex changes the identity

For the triple Q0,Q1,Q2Q_0,Q_1,Q_2, the extra equator vertex is u4u_4. Let A4A_4 be the sum of its two source-face angles, and B4B_4 the sum of its two fan-face angles. Let ξ\xi be the source-face angle at u0u_0 in V4V_4, and ζ\zeta that at u3u_3 in V3V_3. Define

S=(2π−κu0−2ξ)+(2π−κu3−2ζ)>0.S=(2\pi-\kappa_{u_0}-2\xi) +(2\pi-\kappa_{u_3}-2\zeta)>0.

Each summand is strictly positive by the closed spherical-link side bound. The ordinary triangle sums and total curvature give the exact identities

2π+K−2λ−ν=(κq−κp+B4−A4)+S+ν+2ρ,2\pi+K-2\lambda-\nu =(\kappa_q-\kappa_p+B_4-A_4)+S+\nu+2\rho,

2π+K−2ρ−ν=(κq−κp+B4−A4)+S+ν+2λ.2\pi+K-2\rho-\nu =(\kappa_q-\kappa_p+B_4-A_4)+S+\nu+2\lambda.

For a direct derivation, the two outside source triangles give ξ+ζ+A4=2π−ν3−ν4\xi+\zeta+A_4=2\pi-\nu_3-\nu_4; the source sum gives λ+ν+ρ+ν3+ν4=2π−κq\lambda+\nu+\rho+\nu_3+\nu_4=2\pi-\kappa_q; and A4+B4=2π−κu4A_4+B_4=2\pi-\kappa_{u_4}. Substitute these three relations and κq+κp+K+κu0+κu3+κu4=4π\kappa_q+\kappa_p+K+\kappa_{u_0}+\kappa_{u_3}+\kappa_{u_4}=4\pi into SS. No inequality or numerical approximation is used in this step.

Thus the condition

H:=κq−κp+B4−A4≥0H:=\kappa_q-\kappa_p+B_4-A_4\ge0

makes both wider-apex bounds strict and proves the entire selected net in Case A. The integer-coefficient checker expands both identities for all five cyclic triples and both pole choices: 20 exact identities in 20 independent triangle-angle variables.

This answers part of the insertion question quite concretely. At curvature zero, when u4u_4 subdivides the old equator edge u3u0u_3u_0, both A4A_4 and B4B_4 equal π\pi. The correction disappears and the n6 paired-pole identity is recovered. After insertion, the additional term is exactly the difference between the two corner sums at the new vertex.

The correction changes sign when the poles are exchanged. That alone does not finish the proof: the same pole must also satisfy the chain’s combined-extension rule, and after a switch the long patch may change.

Because λ+ρ<π+θ\lambda+\rho<\pi+\theta, both outer angles cannot exceed their common wider bound. The distance-sum inequality also ensures that both copied edges cannot be long. Therefore failure of this separator requires a precise opposing choice: the only short copied edge is at the only oversized apex angle. Equality in either sufficient test is already safe. This is a condition for the test to fail, not proof of overlap.

Four exact coordinate examples, with their replay checker, check the applications and independently separate all 45 face pairs of their selected nets. Random and optimized searches are retained as discovery records; none proves that the remaining small-angle configurations are impossible.

The angle comparisons themselves use rational angle intervals: Machin’s identity bounds π\pi, and an alternating arctangent series after rational argument reduction bounds each face angle. No floating trigonometric value enters a proof predicate. The examples include both signs of HH; a negative HH can still pass the more direct wider-apex test.
