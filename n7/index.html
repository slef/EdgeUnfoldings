A six-to-seven insertion now works with one fixed tree

Planar equator and two poles on the same perpendicular line: the whole geometric family is proved. The heights may differ. Use the pole closer to the equator as the fan, the farther pole as source, and slit at a farthest equator vertex.

Start from a pentagonal pyramid and raise the new pole from its base. The same tree is safe throughout, by comparison with an auxiliary pyramid. This includes an exact four-inward example with both pole curvatures below 0.01°. A separate full-coordinate box certificate also covers nearby shapes with displaced poles and nonplanar equators. A large-curvature pole now settles every bipyramid pattern

If either pole has curvature at least 180°, a suitable edge unfolding exists. The proof works for any equator size, including three and four inward patches. It starts from a shortest-path star and safely replaces its one nonedge cut by an original edge.

A right or obtuse ring corner gives another proved branch, including low-curvature examples. Six exact examples check the constructions. Two exact counterexamples show why the four shortest-patch endpoint choices alone do not finish the general problem.

The remaining receiver pair is now a small-angle question

The small-fan pocket is impossible when a bad patch passes its convex receiver. The whole large-angle branch is proved. For the small-angle branch, the n6 separator extends under an explicit angle test; a new identity isolates the extra term contributed by the seventh vertex.

Four exact examples check the new applications. The pentagonal bipyramid and this whole symbolic class remain open. See the solved branches and the exact remaining condition →

Overnight: a simpler local rule and a larger proved branch

A slit chosen by the largest sum of distances to the poles settles every adjacent patch pair. For seven vertices this leaves five nonadjacent patch pairs: 15 face-pair obligations. Separately, all two-inward-patch patterns have an entire safe net when a pole has curvature at least 180°. Three pattern types also have a stronger weighted-curvature condition that covers examples with both poles below 180°.

The first collision in the earlier minimum-vertex insertion is now classified exactly. Curvature stays ordered during insertion, but that initial tree fails after a precise local cut-line crossing. The whole graph remains open.

A further reduction: one two-patch pattern now has two linked unresolved face pairs for a specified candidate; proving one petal/petal separation settles both. Conflicting pole preferences do occur; an exact integer example has six safe nets and is also covered by a new auxiliary-octahedron argument. Read the remaining metric question →
