Two inward patches: the remaining metric questions

Two inward patches: the genuine remaining obstruction

13 September 2026 · Exact example and a smaller proof target. The two patches can prefer opposite poles, and both can extend beyond their receiving wedge plus the curvature gap. That situation is real, even for a planar pentagonal equator. It does not imply overlap: the exact example below has six independently certified safe star-plus-slit nets.

This example refutes sufficient angular shortcuts, not the existence of an edge unfolding. It shows why the remaining proof must use more of the triangle geometry than the angular cones alone.

How the strict failed inequalities are certified

Each pole has curvature in (0,π)(0,\pi). Multiplying its face-angle complex factors gives the phase of 2π−κp2\pi-\kappa_p; conjugating gives the phase of κp\kappa_p. Multiply by the receiver’s angle factor. Its positive imaginary part certifies that receiver angle plus gap is below π\pi. Both the extension and this bound therefore lie in (0,π)(0,\pi), where cosine is strictly decreasing. The checker proves

cos(receiver angle+κp)−cos(ℓip)>0.\cos(\text{receiver angle}+\kappa_p)-\cos(\ell_i^p)>0.

All coordinates are integers. Square roots and divisions use outward rational intervals; the saved positive margins are recomputed. The rounded angle table is not a proof premise.

2. The opposing-chain class reduces to two face pairs

Now consider every convex pentagonal bipyramid with pattern FCCBC, not just the example. Write ap=∠p(Q1)a_p=\angle_p(Q_1) and bp=∠p(Q2)b_p=\angle_p(Q_2) for the two receiver angles at pole pp.

Two-receiver chain reduction. One can choose a fan pole so that the star-plus-slit net with slit at u0u_0 has at most two unresolved face pairs. If an extension is long, those are its petal against the two triangles of the other receiving patch. Every other pair is proved safe.

First, sum the angle identities at u1,u2,u3u_1,u_2,u_3 and subtract the full angle sums of Q1,Q2Q_1,Q_2. With ρ0=f0−π\rho_0=f_0-\pi and ρ3=e3−π\rho_3=e_3-\pi, the result is

ρ0+ρ3=ω1+ν1+ω2+ν2−κu1−κu2−κu3.\rho_0+\rho_3= \omega_1+\nu_1+\omega_2+\nu_2 -\kappa_{u_1}-\kappa_{u_2}-\kappa_{u_3}.

Since ρi=ℓiv+ℓiw\rho_i=\ell_i^v+\ell_i^w, at least one pole pp satisfies

ℓ0p+ℓ3p<ap+bp.\ell_0^p+\ell_3^p<a_p+b_p.

Call this strict inequality (1).

Choose such a pole and put the slit at pu0p u_0. Both bad patches point into the receiver chain Q1,Q2Q_1,Q_2, so neither extends toward the outer convex patch Q4Q_4 or across this slit.

The two bad cones are disjoint by (1). If both extensions are short (ℓ0p≤ap\ell_0^p\le a_p and ℓ3p≤bp\ell_3^p\le b_p), each meets only its own adjacent receiver, and the entire net is safe.

Otherwise exactly one is long: both cannot be long because that would contradict (1). Suppose ℓ0p>ap\ell_0^p>a_p. Then ℓ3p<bp\ell_3^p<b_p and ℓ0p<ap+bp\ell_0^p<a_p+b_p. The cone of Q0Q_0 can enter Q2Q_2’s fan wedge, but cannot reach the wedge of Q3Q_3. Its fan triangle stays in its own wedge, so only its petal can meet Q2Q_2. The other bad patch reaches only its adjacent receiver Q2Q_2. All other patches are convex.

Adjacent pairs remain safe by their common uncut equator vertex. The only unchecked pairs are therefore

V0/W2,V0/V2,V_0/W_2,\qquad V_0/V_2,

with the notation WW for the chosen pole’s fan and VV for its petals. If ℓ3p>bp\ell_3^p>b_p instead, reflection gives the two unchecked pairs V3/W1V_3/W_1 and V3/V1V_3/V_1.

Thus 43 of 45 pairs are settled for a specifically selected candidate throughout this whole symbolic class. The remaining two are linked metric questions; this count does not establish that most shapes are solved. Equality in an individual short-extension comparison is safe, while (1) is strict because the three intervening vertex curvatures are positive.

The angle-sum identity extends to any chain of convex receivers. The two-pair conclusion here uses exactly two receivers and the pentagonal ring; it should not be read as a proof for an arbitrary longer chain.

The fan pair follows from the petal pair

In fact there is just one independent separation question. Suppose V0/V2V_0/V_2 is safe. The three edges of W2W_2 border, respectively, V2V_2, W1W_1, and W3W_3. The first neighbor is safe by this premise, the second by the common vertex u1u_1, and the third because the two bad cones Q0,Q3Q_0,Q_3 are disjoint under (1). An interior point of V0V_0 cannot lie on any open edge of W2W_2: a small disk inside V0V_0 would overlap the already-safe neighbor on that edge.

The petal V0V_0 has interior points outside W2W_2, near its own equator edge in its own fan wedge. If it also had an interior point inside W2W_2, a generic line segment between two such interior points would cross an open target edge, which was just excluded. Thus V0/W2V_0/W_2 is safe as well. Reflection gives the corresponding implication for V3/W1V_3/W_1.

The count remains 43 pairs already settled, with two linked pairs left; it would be misleading to count the conditional implication as an independent unconditional 44th separation. Proving the one remaining petal/petal pair settles both and completes this pattern’s selected net.

3. An auxiliary octahedron closes a further branch

The six-vertex work can finish the two remaining pairs under explicit, checkable conditions. This is a geometric implication for a family of shapes; the subsequent integer example is only one application.

Auxiliary-octahedron bridge. Use the pole pp from (1), and suppose the long extension is ℓ0p>ap\ell_0^p>a_p. Let qq be the other pole. Assume:

  1. Deleting u4u_4 gives the convex octahedron with equator u0,u1,u2,u3u_0,u_1,u_2,u_3 and poles p,qp,q.
  2. In that six-vertex hull, qq has at least as much curvature as pp.
  3. One of the endpoints u0,u3u_0,u_3 has maximum curvature among that hull’s four equator vertices. All comparisons here concern the reduced hull.

Then the original seven-vertex net from the chain reduction is safe.

Apply the six-vertex paired-pole rule to the auxiliary octahedron: cut the source star at qq and slit at the stated endpoint. Its whole net is nonoverlapping. The three consecutive patches Q0,Q1,Q2Q_0,Q_1,Q_2 are unchanged in the reduced hull. Both endpoint slits leave their internal fan joins pu1,pu2pu_1,pu_2 uncut, and all three equator hinges remain uncut. Their connected developed placement is therefore congruent to their placement in the original seven-vertex candidate.

In particular the auxiliary theorem separates V0V_0 from both triangles of Q2Q_2. These are exactly the two remaining pairs. The chain reduction settles every other pair in the original net, proving the conclusion. The auxiliary closing edge u3u0u_3u_0 is not used as a cut or inserted into the original net; it serves only to justify those two face separations.

For a long Q3Q_3, reflection uses deletion of u0u_0 and auxiliary equator u1,u2,u3,u4u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4, with an endpoint maximum at u1u_1 or u4u_4. Ties in the auxiliary source and slit comparisons are allowed by the six-vertex theorem. This bridge currently assumes an actual octahedral reduced hull; other deletion types need separate arguments.

So the example that defeats both earlier cone shortcuts is covered by a proved family-level bridge as well as by its six individual safe-net certificates.

The paired-pole theorem is a written n6 dependency, undergoing separate formalization. Its files are not changed by this argument, and the n7 checker does not claim to formalize that theorem.

4. Next proof question

When ℓ0p>ap\ell_0^p>a_p, prove separation of V0V_0 from the whole convex patch Q2Q_2, or show that the other pole or a different slit repairs any failure. The auxiliary bridge already does this when its three geometric conditions hold. The remaining branches are: a different reduced graph, the wrong reduced pole ordering, or an interior equator maximum. Check which of those can coexist with the chain’s one-long-extension condition; do not assume the auxiliary hypotheses merely from six-vertex existence.

The other two distinct-receiver classes and adjacent same-direction patches remain separate targets. The example above belongs to the opposing-chain class and does not refute a more specific pole-choice rule for either of those other classes.
